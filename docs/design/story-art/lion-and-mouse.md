# The Lion and the Mouse: art record

Store story (`StoreTexts.LionAndMouse`, stem `lion-and-mouse`, version 5). Retold as 15 pages with one
action each (`dino-storytelling` skill). Version 5 (Slice 146 Wave A, 2026-10-26): Farsi rewritten in spoken Farsi, sound words as plain triples; English, page breaks and every picture unchanged. **Art status (2026-10-26): cover and all 15 pages drawn, checked at full size and prepared** into `tests/DinoReader.Tests/Store/lion-and-mouse/` (full review PASS 2026-10-26 after the p7, p8 and p14 redraws).

**Gentle retelling (Decision 130):** in Aesop the Lion is caught in a hunters' net and roars in fear. Here
there are no hunters, no trap and nobody in danger: an old fishing net hangs between two trees by the
river, drying in the sun. The Lion, bouncing after a butterfly and not looking where he goes (just as the
Mouse did not look on page 1), runs into it and gets tangled. He is stuck, cross and silly-looking
("This is no fun at all!"), never hurt, frightened or squeezed: the net is loose and wrapped around him,
not pulled tight. In the classic the Lion's paw traps the Mouse; here he puts it down *beside* her, so
she can't run on, and nobody threatens to eat anyone.

**Why everyone does what they do:** the Mouse runs over the Lion's nose because she is hurrying home and
does not see him; the Lion is grumpy because she woke him; he lets her go because she is sorry and he is
a kind king, and laughs because her promise sounds so funny from someone so small; he runs into the net
because he is chasing a butterfly; the Mouse comes because she hears his grumpy roar, knows it, and
remembers her promise ("He sounds stuck! Now it is my turn to be kind to him"); she can help because her little teeth can
nibble rope; the Lion says sorry for laughing at her, and the Mouse answers "Kind friends help each other"; the ending pays the opening back (she ran over his nose, now she sleeps on his paw).

**Refrain:** "Pitter, patter, pitter, patter, went her little feet." on pages 1, 5 and 10 (running home,
running home again, running to help), identical every time. "Nibble, nibble, snip!" twice on page 12.

**Style:** Children's picture book illustration, soft watercolour and coloured pencil, warm gentle
colours, simple rounded shapes, no text or letters anywhere. Portrait 3:4.

## Characters (accepted line-up: `docs/design/story-art/lion-and-mouse/characters/lineup.jpg`, one prompt on 3.8 Flash, no separate sheets)

Confirmed against the accepted line-up. Differences from the first plan: the Mouse's dress has a small white collar and her berry looks like a small strawberry (red with a green top).

- **Lion:** a large, friendly lion, by far the biggest character (the Mouse is about as long as one of his
  front paws is wide). Warm golden-yellow fur, a big fluffy russet-brown mane, a tuft of brown at the tip
  of his tail, round amber eyes, cream muzzle and tummy. No clothes. His prop: a small gold crown with
  three rounded points, sitting tilted on his mane (he is "the king of the beasts"); it stays on in every
  picture, even inside the net. Never shows his teeth or claws: grumpy means a frown, a pout or a
  closed-mouth "grr"; a roar is drawn as a round open "O" mouth with no teeth showing.
- **Mouse:** a tiny grey-brown field mouse, standing about as tall as the Lion's nose is wide; big round
  pink ears, a pink nose, long whiskers, a long thin pink tail. She wears a little blue dress with white
  dots and nothing else. Her prop: a red berry she carries on pages 1-5 (on her way home with her supper);
  from page 6 on she has empty paws. Nibbling rope shows two small front teeth, as mice do; nothing else
  about her is sharp.
- **The butterfly (page 6 only, not a character):** one small yellow butterfly, no face.

There are no other characters: no hunters, no people anywhere, no other animals except the page-6
butterfly.

## Places (the same in every picture)

- **The Lion's tree:** a big shady tree with a wide flat-topped crown (like an acacia), alone in warm,
  long golden grass under a pale blue sky. Pages 1-5 and 15.
- **The Mouse's nest:** a cosy round nest of dry grass tucked between the roots of a small bush at the
  edge of the long grass, far from the river. Page 9.
- **The river:** a slow, shallow, sparkling river with a grassy bank and round stones. Two slim trees stand
  a few lion-lengths apart on the bank, and between them hangs an **old fishing net**: soft brown rope with
  big square holes, sagging loosely like a hammock, no floats, hooks or weights. Pages 6-8 and 11-14.
- **The long grass between them:** golden grass taller than the Mouse, with a few small flowers. Page 10.

## Picture plan

Every page: the whole scene sits in the top 70% of the picture; the bottom 30% is plain grass, ground or
riverbank with nothing important in it. No text, letters, words, sound effects or writing anywhere ("Bump!",
"Grr!", "Pitter, patter" and "snip" are never drawn in). Every page is **new** (no pictures exist).

Notes for the illustrator:

- Pages 2-5 share the tree and both characters; tell them apart by the action (2: paw comes down, Lion
  frowning; 3: Lion on his front, head low, listening, Mouse pleading; 4: Lion on his front, head thrown
  back laughing; 5: paw lifted high while the Mouse runs off). The paw stays flat beside the Mouse on 2-4
  and is lifted only on 5. Keep the berry on pages 1-5 only.
- Pages 7-8 and 11-14 are at the same two trees and net; the net keeps the same look, and from page 12 on
  it shows loose, nibbled ropes and, from page 13, a big hole.
- The net never looks like a trap or a cage: soft, loose, old, sagging; no hunters, people, spears, ropes
  pulled tight around his neck or anything sharp. The Lion is never upside down or hanging off the ground.
- Size: the Mouse is tiny next to the Lion, so on pages 2-5 and 11-14 draw her close to the viewer or near
  his face so she is still easy to see.
- The Lion's mouth: closed, smiling, or a round toothless "O" when he roars; no teeth or claws. Ask for it
  in every prompt ("mouth closed or smiling, no teeth showing, no claws").
- Ask for "no text, letters, words, sound effects or writing anywhere".
