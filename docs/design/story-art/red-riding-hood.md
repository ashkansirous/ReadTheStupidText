# Little Red Riding Hood: art record

Store story (`StoreTexts.RedRidingHood`, stem `red-riding-hood`, id `1f0f0cb207b244368b7aef26028f4ac5`, version 4).
Retold as 14 pages with one action each (`dino-storytelling` skill, Slice 121). **Art status (2026-10-26): cover and
pages 1-14 drawn and accepted; full review PASS 2026-10-26.** The old 9-page text-only version had none.

**Gentle retelling (Decision 130):** in the classic the Wolf tricks Red Riding Hood into leaving the path, runs
ahead, swallows Grandmother (or locks her away), dresses in her nightclothes to fool the girl and leaps at her,
and the Woodcutter cuts him open or chases him off. None of that is here. The Wolf is a young wolf who is new to
the wood, lost, hungry and lonely. He never lies, threatens, chases, hides or dresses up to fool anyone, and
nobody is ever in danger or frightened. His one ask (come and pick blackberries among the trees) is the classic
"pick flowers in the meadow" detour made honest: he wants a friend, and he takes her "No, thank you" at once.
Red Riding Hood does what her mother asked: she stays on the path, says "No, thank you", and tells a grown-up
she knows (the Woodcutter, Grandma's friend). The grown-up goes back with her, finds out the Wolf is lost, and
invites him to Grandma's birthday party. Grandma welcomes everyone ("There's always room for one more!") and
they share the basket. "What big eyes you have!" stays as a game at the party table, with everybody laughing:
the Wolf asks to try on Grandma's spare glasses (big eyes), then tries her fluffy pink earmuffs from the peg
by the door, much too small for his ears (big ears). **"What big teeth you have!" is dropped**; the third step is "What a big smile you have!",
answered "That's because I'm not lost any more. I've found some friends!" The Woodcutter never holds an axe
or any tool (he stacks logs), and nothing in the cottage burns or steams: no fireplace, candles or teapot.
The ending is bedtime: Red Riding Hood tells Mother she stayed on the path and told a grown-up, and the Wolf
comes to Grandma's for honey cake every Sunday.

**Why everyone does what they do:**
- **Mother** packs the basket because it is Grandma's birthday. She asks Red Riding Hood to stay on the path
  and gives her the rule for strangers (say "no, thank you" and tell a grown-up you know), because the wood is
  big and she wants her to get there the right way.
- **Red Riding Hood** says her rule back as a happy chant ("I stay on the path, all the way to Grandma's!"),
  so she remembers it. She says no to the Wolf politely because he is a stranger asking her to leave the path,
  not because he is scary, and she tells the Woodcutter because he is a grown-up she knows.
- **The Wolf** sits by the path because he is new in the wood and cannot find his way; he asks her to pick
  blackberries because he is hungry and has nobody to play with. He accepts her "no" (his ears droop, he
  stays on his log). At the party he asks before he tries on Grandma's glasses ("Can I try them on?" "Of
  course!"), the earmuffs hang on the peg by the door, and he
  smiles because he is not lost or alone any more.
- **The Woodcutter** praises her for staying on the path and telling him, then goes back with her (holding
  her hand) because a grown-up should see whether the Wolf needs help. He invites the Wolf because Grandma
  "always says there's room for one more".
- **Grandma** opens the door to everyone because that is what she always says, says yes when the Wolf asks to
  try her glasses, and hugs him because he is her new friend.
- **Button** the puppy goes everywhere with Red Riding Hood (so she is never quite alone in the wood, and every
  wood picture has three characters). Button never speaks and never barks at the Wolf.

**Refrains (identical every time):**
- `"I stay on the path, all the way to Grandma's!"` said or sung by Red Riding Hood on pages 2, 5 and 8 (the
  promise, the "No, thank you", the walk with her new friends).
- `Tip, tap, tip, tap, went her little red boots along the path.` on pages 3 and 6.
- `Rumble, rumble, rumble!` (the Wolf's tummy) on pages 4 and 7.
- `"There's always room for one more"`: the Woodcutter quotes Grandma on page 8, Grandma says it on page 9.
- The game makes the three-step build-up: `"Wolf, what big eyes you have!"` / `"All the better to see you
  with!"` (page 11), `"Wolf, what big ears you have!"` / `"All the better to hear you with!"` (page 12), then
  the different last step `"Wolf, what a big smile you have!"` (page 13). Pages 11 and 12 end with the same
  `Ha, ha, ha! Hee, hee, hee! Everybody laughed.`

**Cast and voices:** Red Riding Hood, Mother, Wolf, Grandma, Woodcutter (`StoreTexts` spec list). Genders,
chosen on purpose: Red Riding Hood, Mother and Grandma are female; the Wolf and the Woodcutter are male. The
caster reads them from the titles ("Mother", "Grandma"), the pronouns after the names ("she", "his"), and the
Wolf's name, which casts the roster's wolf. The Woodcutter's pronoun cue is page 7 ("The Woodcutter put down
his logs. He took Red Riding Hood's hand"); an earlier draft ("took her hand, and he went back with her")
counted more "her" than "he" and gave him a witch's voice in every language. Button never speaks, so is not
in the cast. `RedRidingHoodStoryTests.LittleRedRidingHood_GivesEachCharacterAVoiceOfTheirGender_InEveryLanguagesRoster`
checks all 14 languages. Translations keep the genders: Arabic قالت for the girls and women, قال for the Wolf
and the Woodcutter, feminine imperatives to Red Riding Hood; Russian сказала / сказал, and Пуговка is a
feminine «собачка» so her verbs agree; French "le Petit Chaperon rouge" takes "elle" and "tu es restée".

**Translations (Decision 152 register):** all 13 languages written fresh for this text in the spoken,
kid-friendly register: Farsi colloquial forms throughout (می‌مونم، بزرگ‌تر، گشنمه، قار و قور), French in
the passé composé with "a dit" tags, Brazilian Portuguese (tô, pra, a gente), Spanish from Spain (as the other
store books: "¡Pasad, pasad!"), Japanese and Chinese with no spaces. Each language uses its own name for the
heroine (شنل قرمزی, ذات الرداء الأحمر, Красная Шапочка, le Petit Chaperon rouge, Caperucita Roja, Chapeuzinho
Vermelho, Roodkapje, Rødhætte, Rödluvan, Rødhette, Punahilkka, 赤ずきんちゃん, 小红帽) and its own sound words
(تق!, Тук!, Toc !, Klop!, Knack!, Kop!, トン！, 咚！ for the knock; قار و قور, Урр, Gargouille, Ronc, Kurr,
グー, 咕噜, Gruuu, Rommel for the tummy; never a growl word like "Grrr" or "Grommel", which would make the Wolf sound menacing). Button is translated as a name (دکمه, زُرَير, Пуговка, Bouton, Botón, Botão,
Knoopje, Knap, Knappen, Nappi, ボタン, 纽扣).

**Style:** Children's picture book illustration, soft watercolour and coloured pencil, warm gentle
colours, simple rounded shapes, no text or letters anywhere. Portrait 3:4.

## Characters (canonical until a line-up is accepted; then rewrite from the line-up)

Original looks for this book: none of them may echo a famous film or cartoon (no Disney Big Bad Wolf with
patched trousers and hat, no Tex Avery wolf in a suit, no Shrek wolf in a nightgown, no "Hoodwinked" characters).

- **Red Riding Hood:** a girl of about six, the main character; round face, rosy cheeks, dark brown curly
  hair in two short bunches. A **bright red hooded cape** to her knees, tied at the neck with a red bow (hood up
  outdoors, down indoors), over a **yellow dress with small white dots**, and **little red boots**. Her prop:
  a wicker basket with a **blue-and-white checked cloth** (pages 2-10). In bed (page 14) a plain white
  nightdress; the red cape hangs on a hook by the bed.
- **Mother:** a slim young woman, a head and a half taller than Red Riding Hood; the same dark brown curly
  hair, in a bun tied with a green ribbon; a **sage-green dress** with a **white apron with one small red heart
  on the pocket**. No other props.
- **Wolf:** a young grey wolf, big and soft-looking: sitting, his head is level with Red Riding Hood's head;
  standing on four legs, his back reaches her shoulder. Fluffy grey fur, a pale cream chest and muzzle, big
  pointy ears, a bushy grey tail, round friendly amber eyes. He walks on four legs and sits on his haunches
  (on a chair at the table). He wears only a **mustard-yellow neckerchief** and carries a **small brown
  satchel on a strap across his body** (he is a traveller, new to the wood). No trousers, no shirt, no
  waistcoat, no hat, no nightcap, no nightgown, no clothes of Grandma's except the page-11 glasses and the
  page-12 earmuffs. Mouth always closed or smiling, **no teeth showing**, no drool, no tongue out.
- **Woodcutter:** a tall, broad, friendly man, the tallest character; a short red-brown beard, rosy cheeks;
  a **green-and-brown checked shirt**, brown trousers with braces, brown boots and a **green woolly bobble
  hat**. His prop: an armful of short logs (page 6) or empty hands. **Never an axe, saw, knife or any tool**,
  anywhere in the book.
- **Grandma:** a short, round, smiling old woman, a little taller than Red Riding Hood; white hair in a bun,
  her own **small round gold-rimmed glasses** (always on), a **lilac cardigan** over a long blue skirt, and a
  **pink flower brooch** (it is her birthday). Her spare glasses are big, round and **red-framed** (page 11,
  on the Wolf); her earmuffs are **fluffy pink** (page 12, on the Wolf).
- **Button (not a speaker):** a small fluffy **white puppy**, knee-high to Red Riding Hood, with **one brown
  ear (her left)**, a **red collar** and a curly tail. Never barks at, growls at or chases the Wolf.

There are no other characters: no hunters, no other wolves, no other animals with faces.

## Places (the same in every picture)

- **Home:** a little white cottage with a red door and a wooden garden gate, at the edge of an autumn wood.
  Inside, a sunny kitchen with a wooden table and one wide window with a single pane. No stove, fireplace,
  candle or anything burning. Pages 1-3. Red Riding Hood's bedroom (page 14): a wooden bed with a patchwork
  quilt, the red cape on a hook, moonlight through one wide single-pane window; no lamp, candle or flame.
- **The path through the wood:** a winding earth path through a bright autumn wood, orange and gold leaves,
  sunshine. Off the path among the trees, a few **blackberry bushes**. A **fallen log** lies beside the path
  in the middle of the wood (pages 4, 5, 7). Further along, the Woodcutter's **neat log pile** beside the path
  (page 6).
- **Grandma's cottage:** a little stone cottage with a **blue door** under a big old oak tree with golden
  leaves (page 9). Inside (pages 10-13): a cosy room, a small round wooden table with a blue-checked
  tablecloth, the open basket, a plate of honey cakes, red apples and a jug of milk. **No teapot, no steam, no
  fireplace, no candles (not even on a cake).** A peg by the door.

## Picture plan

or floor with nothing important in it. No text, letters, words, sound effects or writing anywhere ("Tip, tap",
"Rumble", "Knock", "Yum" and "Ha, ha" are never drawn in, and no music notes). Nobody looks scared. Every page
is **new** (no pictures exist). Every picture has at least three characters.

The last item is ticked for the plan: every look is fixed in the character lines above, and the illustrator
draws one line-up from them (all six, Button included) before any page.

Notes for the illustrator:

- Every page has three or more characters, partly because Gemini refuses some one- or two-character scenes;
  a girl alone with a wolf in a wood is the scene most likely to be refused, so pages 4 and 5 always include
  Button and say "a friendly picture-book scene, nobody is in danger".
- The Wolf: four legs, soft and friendly, mustard neckerchief and brown satchel only. Every prompt with him
  says "mouth closed or smiling, no teeth showing" and "no trousers, no shirt, no hat, no nightcap, no
  nightgown". On pages 11 and 12 he wears only the glasses or only the earmuffs, never both.
- The Woodcutter: every prompt says "no axe, no saw, no knife, no tools of any kind".
- Grandma's room: every prompt says "no fireplace, no candles, no teapot, no steam, no flames".
- Pages 10-13 share the room and differ by action: sharing cake, glasses, earmuffs (by the door peg), the hug.
  Button has one fixed small place on all four: curled on the same little stool beside Red Riding Hood's
  chair. Pages 9-13 are the biggest cast of any Dino book (five): every prompt says "exactly five characters,
  each appearing once"; if Flash-Lite drops or doubles someone twice, redo that page in a fresh chat before
  anyone changes the text. Keep the
  glasses red-framed (Grandma's own stay gold, on her face) so they cannot be confused.
- Page 8: one picture of everyone walking as they set off; don't try to show the invitation at the log too.
  No music notes for the singing.
- Ask for "exactly N characters, each appearing once" with the count from the table, and "no text, letters,
  words, sound effects or writing anywhere".
