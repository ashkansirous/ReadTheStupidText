**Gentle retelling (Decision 130):** in the classic Jack trades the family cow for beans, his mother throws them
out in anger, and Jack climbs to the Giant's castle three times to steal a bag of gold, the hen that lays golden
eggs and the singing harp, while the Giant roars "Fee-fi-fo-fum, I smell the blood of an Englishman... I'll grind
his bones to make my bread", chases him, and falls to his death when Jack chops the beanstalk down. None of that
is here. **Nobody steals, chases, chops, falls or is frightened, and nothing is sold that the family loves:** the
old cow Milky-White stays at home. Jack goes to market to buy seeds with the last coin and the Peddler, a cheerful
seed-seller, offers him magic beans instead. Mother laughs ("beans are seeds too") and plants them with him. The
Giant's hen has wandered down the beanstalk into Jack's garden, so Jack's one climb is to **take her home**, the
classic theft turned inside out. He asks first, and Mother says "Let's take her home together": she ties a basket
on his back so his hands are free, gives him the safety rule and climbs right behind him (text review, 2026-10-26:
a child climbing alone to a stranger's home is what a 3-year-old would copy). The Giant is big, kind, lonely, clumsy and far too loud: his "Fee, fi, fo, fum!" is
a friendly hello song that makes the birds fly away. Jack teaches him to sing it softly; the birds come back. The
Giant shares one of Goldie's golden eggs with Mother there, which buys seeds for the whole garden, and asks them to
visit every Sunday, "both of you" (no gift-and-come-back-alone from a stranger). Jack and Mother climb down as
carefully as they went up, the Peddler is waiting at the bottom and Mother hugs Jack; the beanstalk is never cut
and nobody falls. The ending is
bedtime: the Giant sings a soft goodnight from the clouds.

**Why everyone does what they do:**
- **Mother** sends Jack for seeds because a garden will fill their empty cupboard. She laughs at five beans
  kindly, not crossly, because beans are seeds too. She agrees to the climb because the hen must go home, and
  she makes it safe: she comes too, climbing right behind him, with a basket on his back (both hands free) and
  the rule "Hold on with both hands, one step at a time". She is beside Jack in the cloud land, so every
  meeting with the Giant and his gift happen with a grown-up there; at the bottom she hugs him.
- **Jack** buys the beans because the Peddler says they are better than seeds (the classic "bargain", made
  honest). He climbs because "somebody up there must be missing her": he is giving something back, not taking.
  He says the safety rule to himself on the way up and down. He giggles and puts his fingers in his ears (so does Mother)
  because the Giant is loud, not because anyone is scared, and he offers to help because the Giant is lonely.
- **The Peddler** is a kind, slightly magical seed-seller: she gives Jack the beans fairly for his coin, tells
  him what to do with them, and is waiting at the foot of the beanstalk when Jack and Mother climb down ("I told
  you they were magic beans").
- **The Giant** sings his hello loudly because that is the only way he knows; the birds fly off and "nobody
  ever comes to play", so he is lonely. He is clumsy (his sigh blows Jack's cap off; he catches it, says
  sorry and explains "Everything about me is too big and too loud"). He is so glad to have Goldie back that he wants to share, and once Jack has taught him to sing softly
  he is careful and gentle (he puts the egg in the basket "very gently").
- **Goldie** (the hen, never speaks) wandered down the beanstalk and got lost; she is why Jack climbs.
- **Milky-White** (the cow) and the **donkey** never speak; they make the home and market pictures three-character
  scenes. The cow is never sold.

**Refrains (identical every time):**
- `"Hold on with both hands, one step at a time," said Mother.` on page 6, and Jack's `"Hold on with both hands, one step at a time, Mum," said Jack.` on pages 7 (going
  up, Mother right behind him) and 13 (Jack, going down with Mother). The safety model of the book.
- `Cluck, cluck, cluck!` (Goldie) on pages 5, 7 and 9.
- The Giant's song: `"Fee, fi, fo, fum! Hello, hello, everyone!"` loud on page 8 (the birds fly away),
  whispered on page 11 (the birds come back), and the different last step on page 15,
  `"Fee, fi, fo, fum! Goodnight, goodnight, everyone!"`, sung "as softly as a mouse" (the phrase Jack taught him
  on page 11). No "I smell a boy" and nothing about bones.
- Sounds made long enough to hear, each one piece and a triple (Decision 151): `Moo, moo, moo!`, `Rustle, rustle, rustle!`, `Boom, boom, boom!` (his
  footsteps, page 8 only), `rattle, rattle, rattle!` (the windows, page 9), `Whoosh, whoosh, whoosh!` (his sigh, page 10). Every translation
  repeats each sound three times too (French «Boum, boum, boum !», Russian «Бум! Бум! Бум!», Japanese «ドシン、ドシン、ドシン！», Chinese «咚，咚，咚！»).
  Untested aloud outside English, Spanish, Chinese and Farsi: flag the new rattle triples and the non-measured languages for a listen.

**Cast and voices (Slice 156, the book's own choice, `StarterVoices` entry `StoreTexts.BeanstalkId`):** Jack, Mother, Giant, Peddler
(`StoreTexts` spec list). Genders, chosen on purpose: Jack and the Giant are male; Mother and the Peddler are female (the Peddler is a
woman so the book has two of each, and her pronoun cue comes straight after her name: "a funny little Peddler. She had a cart").

- **Jack:** suggested `piglet` (Cheeky Piglet, small and quick). Traits: male, child, person; bright, bouncy. A boy of seven, not a grown man.
- **Mother:** suggested `mummy` (Kind Mummy, warm and kind). Traits: female, adult, person; warm, soft.
- **Giant:** suggested `giant` (Friendly Giant, big, deep and slow). Traits: male, adult, giant; booming, warm. Big and kind, never gruff.
- **Peddler:** suggested `bee` (Busy Bee, bubbly and quick). Traits: female, old, person; bouncy, bright. A funny little old seed-seller who winks.

The picker had made Jack the Brave Knight (a grown man), the Giant the Rumbly Dragon and the Peddler the Brave Princess, whose slot is a
whisper in Spanish, Portuguese and Farsi. The real cast was dumped for all 14 languages (the `StoryVoicing.CastIn` path): every
suggestion is kept in every language, everyone gets their own gender, no two characters share a voice and flavour, nobody is on a
whisper flavour, and the Giant is on the Giant flavour everywhere. Farsi has three voices, so Jack (gyro, Chirpy) and the Giant (gyro,
Giant) share a voice with different flavours, and Mother and the Peddler share mana with the narrator (Normal, Small, Storyteller).

checks all 14 languages. Goldie, Milky-White, the donkey and the birds never speak, so they are not in the cast.

**Translations (Decision 152 register):** all 13 languages written fresh for this text in the spoken,

**Style:** Children's picture book illustration, soft watercolour and coloured pencil, warm gentle
colours, simple rounded shapes, no text or letters anywhere. Portrait 3:4.

## Characters (canonical until a line-up is accepted; then rewrite from the line-up)

Original looks for this book: none may echo a famous film or cartoon (no Disney "Mickey and the Beanstalk"
Willie the Giant in a green suit, no DreamWorks/Shrek ogre, no "Jack the Giant Slayer" two-headed giants, no
Roald Dahl BFG with huge ears and a waistcoat, no Studio Ghibli cloud castle).

- **Jack:** a boy of about seven, the main character; freckles, a round face, a mop of **straw-blond hair**. A
  **sky-blue tunic** with a brown belt over **patched brown trousers** (one green patch on the left knee), brown
  boots, and a small **red cap** (blown off on page 10, back on from page 11). His prop: a **wicker basket on two
  straps worn on his back** (pages 6-14); on pages 9 and 12 he holds it in front of him. In bed (page 15) plain
  white pyjamas; the red cap hangs on the bedpost.
- **Mother:** a slim woman, a head taller than Jack; the same straw-blond hair in a long plait; a **rose-pink
  dress** with a **cream apron** and rolled-up sleeves; a **yellow headscarf**. Prop: a small green watering can
  (page 3 only).
- **Peddler:** a short, round, cheerful old woman, about Jack's height; rosy cheeks, white curly hair, round
  spectacles; a **purple shawl** over a **mustard-yellow skirt**, and a **floppy green hat with one long orange
  feather**. Her prop: a **little wooden cart** with lots of small seed packets and cloth bags (no writing on
  them), pulled by the donkey.
- **The donkey (not a speaker):** small, sleepy-looking, soft grey with a white muzzle and a **red blanket**;
  always half-closed eyes. Pages 2 and 14.
- **Milky-White (not a speaker):** an old, gentle **cream-white cow with a few pale brown spots** and a **small
  brass bell** on a leather collar; no horns. Pages 1, 3 and 4.
- **Goldie (not a speaker):** a plump little hen, the size of a cat; **shiny golden feathers**, a small red comb,
  bright black eyes. Pages 5-13 and cover. Her golden egg is the size of a hen's egg in Jack's hands.
- **The Giant:** a man as tall as a big tree (standing, Jack reaches his knee; sitting on his castle step, his
  face is high above Jack). Big, round, friendly face, rosy cheeks, a **bushy chestnut-brown beard and eyebrows**,
  small kind eyes, **bald on top**. A **patchwork tunic of soft green and blue squares**, a wide brown belt,
  **sky-grey trousers**, enormous brown boots. No crown, no club, no weapon, no armour, no horns, no tusks; mouth
  closed or smiling (singing: an open "O"), **no teeth showing**. He is never angry.
- **Two bluebirds (not speakers):** small, round, bright blue with white tummies. Page 7 (flying past), 8
  (flying away), 11 (landing on his shoulder).

## Places (the same in every picture)

- **Home:** a little thatched cottage with a **blue door**, one wide single-pane kitchen window and a low wooden
  fence; a bare vegetable patch under the window; a green meadow and a wooden gate. Pages 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 14.
  The **beanstalk** (from page 4): a huge twisting green stalk as wide as a tree trunk, big heart-shaped leaves
  like steps, curling tendrils, rising into the clouds from the vegetable patch under the kitchen window.
- **The market:** a sunny village square with a stone well and a few striped awnings, nobody else in it
  (no extra people). Page 2.
- **Up the beanstalk:** blue sky, treetops and the cottage roof small below, soft white clouds above. Pages 7
  and 13.
- **The cloud land:** a road of soft white cloud, a pale stone castle with round towers, one huge round-topped
  wooden door, wide single-pane windows and a broad stone step; golden sunshine. Pages 8-13.
- **Jack's bedroom:** a small wooden bed with a patchwork quilt, one wide single-pane window with moonlight;
  outside, the beanstalk rising into silvery clouds, with the tiny castle and the tiny Giant on top (no face in the
  sky or moon). No lamp, candle or flame. Page 15.

Notes for the illustrator:

- Every page has three or more characters (Gemini refuses some one- or two-character scenes). On page 15 the
  third is the Giant, tiny and far off in the clouds through the window (smiling, eyes soft, no face in the
  sky itself); keep him small so the bedroom stays calm, and never add a third person in the room.
- Scale is the hard part: the Giant must look huge but soft and friendly. On pages 8-12 say "a kind, smiling
  giant, as tall as a tree, Jack reaches his knee", "mouth closed or smiling, no teeth showing", "no weapon, no
  club, no crown, no armour", and "nobody is scared, a friendly picture-book scene".
- Page 13 is drawn from above (the Giant at the cloud's edge, Jack and then Mother climbing below him). Both must be holding the
  stalk with both hands; never let either look as if they are slipping or falling, and keep the drop soft (clouds and
  leaves, not a sheer view of the ground).
- Page 7 likewise: Jack and Mother both have both hands on the stalk, one foot on a leaf, smiling, the ground far but soft.
- Page 2: the market has no other people; "exactly three characters: the boy, the old woman and the donkey".
- Mother is in every picture from page 6 to 14 (she climbs with Jack); the same outfit each time, apron and headscarf
  included, never a second Mother. Pages 8, 10 and 11: the cap is red on Jack's head on 8, in the Giant's fingers on 10, back on Jack's head on 11.
- Pages 9-12 share the castle step and differ by action: Goldie flies to the Giant, the cap, the finger-on-lips
  lesson with the birds, the egg.
- Ask for "exactly N characters, each appearing once" with the count from the table, and "no text, letters,
  words, sound effects or writing anywhere" (seed packets and the market have no labels).
