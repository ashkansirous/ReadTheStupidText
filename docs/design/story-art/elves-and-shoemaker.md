# The Elves and the Shoemaker: art record

Store story (`StoreTexts.Elves`, stem `elves-and-shoemaker`, version 5). Retold as 15 pages with one action each
(`dino-storytelling` skill, Slice 121). **Art status (2026-10-26): all 16 pictures drawn and prepared; full review PASSED on 2026-10-26.** The age range went from 5-8 to 4-8 because the reviewer advised it for the gentler text. The cover and all
15 pages are new; the text-only version 4 had none.

**Gentle retelling (Decision 130):** in the Grimms' tale the shoemaker is so poor he has leather for one last pair
and nothing else; the couple hide behind a curtain at night to spy on the elves, who are naked; when the elves find
the clothes they sing that they are "too fine to be cobblers" and leave for good, never to return. Here:
- **Nobody is poor in a frightening way.** Hans and Greta are tired at the end of a winter's day and the leather has
  run low ("only enough leather left for one more pair"). There is no hunger, no empty house, no worry about money;
  the first sale simply means "Hans could buy leather for two more pairs!".
- **Nobody spies.** Greta wants to find out who is helping **so they can say thank you**. They sit openly in the big
  armchair in the corner of the workshop under a blanket, with the cat, in the moonlight, and Hans tries not to yawn.
  When the shoes are done they walk over and thank the elves face to face; the elves giggle and say they love making
  shoes for kind people.
- **The elves are never naked.** They wear thin, patched little tunics and have bare feet, which is why Greta and Hans
  make them warm coats, scarves and boots. The gift is warm and given openly ("Yes, they're all for you!").
- **The elves are not driven away.** They dance in their new clothes, say thank you, and leave happily and by
  choice: now that they are warm they can go and help other kind people. The book ends right there at the shop door
  in the moonlight (one moment, page 15): Hans and Greta wave, and Greta says goodbye with the closing thought, "When
  someone is kind to you, you can be kind to someone else." (Text review 2026-10-26: an earlier "snug in bed" epilogue
  was a second moment and was cut, since store stories stop at 15 pages.)
- Rule 1 details: **no fireplace, stove, candle, lamp flame or fire anywhere**; night light is moonlight through the
  window. Hans cuts leather with big shoemaker's scissors, never a knife. The elves' tools are a tiny hammer and a tiny
  needle. "Christmas" is left out (the tale works as plain winter for every family).

**Why everyone does what they do:**
- **Hans** cuts out the last leather in the evening so it is ready to sew in the morning; he is too tired to sew it
  now. He says "It's our turn to help them" because the elves helped first, and he makes the tiny boots because
  shoes are what he is best at.
- **Greta** has the idea of staying up because "somebody is being very kind to us" and she wants to say thank you,
  not to catch anyone. She saw the elves' bare feet and thin tunics on page 9, so she sews the coats and scarves to
  keep their toes "warm and cosy".
- **Nib and Nob** help because Hans and Greta are kind to everyone who comes into their shop (page 1), and the
  elves "love making shoes for kind people" (page 11). They come in through the cat flap because it is just their
  size. They leave on page 15 because, now that they are warm, they can travel through the snow to help other kind
  people too; it is a happy goodbye, not being sent away.
- **The lady in the purple coat** pays a very good price because the shoes fit just right and are the finest she has
  seen; that is how the leather grows from one pair to two, then more and more.

**Refrain (identical every time):** `Tap, tap, tap! Stitch, stitch, stitch!` on pages 3 and 6 (narration, the sound
heard from upstairs), 10 (`"Tap, tap, tap!" sang Nib` / `"Stitch, stitch, stitch!" sang Nob`: the same two pieces,
sung by the elves as they work). Finnish uses `Tikki, tikki, tikki!` for the stitch (review: "Pistä" sounds like "poke"). The build-up is one pair (page 2), two pairs
(page 5), then more and more (page 6).

**Cast and voices:** Hans, Greta, Nib, Nob (`StoreTexts` spec list). Genders are chosen two and two: **Hans and Nob
are "he", Greta and Nib are "she"** (set by the pronouns: "Hans lived with his wife", "the smallest he had ever made",
"sang Nib, with her tiny hammer", "Nib twirled round in her new red coat", "sang Nob, with his tiny needle", "said
Nob, and he waved", "Nob stamped his tiny new boots"). None of them has an animal word, so no `StarterAnimals` entry is
needed: each is cast by gender. `StoreStoryTests.TheElvesAndTheShoemaker_GivesEachCharacterAVoiceOfTheirGender_InEveryLanguagesRoster`
checks the stored genders and all 14 languages. The cat and the lady never speak, so they get no voice. Translations
keep each gender: Russian сказал Ганс / сказала Грета / сказала Нибб / сказал Ноб; Arabic قال هانس / قالت غريتا /
قالت نيب / قال نوب; Finnish Nipsu and Nopsu, Norwegian Nipp and Napp, Dutch Greet (names kept from version 4).

**Style:** Children's picture book illustration, soft watercolour and coloured pencil, warm gentle colours, simple
rounded shapes, no text or letters anywhere. Portrait 3:4.

## Characters (canonical until a line-up is accepted; then rewrite from the line-up)

- **Hans:** the shoemaker, a tall, friendly man, the tallest in the book; short **grey-brown beard**, round
  **wire glasses**, rosy cheeks. He wears a **mustard-yellow shirt with rolled-up sleeves, brown trousers and a
  dark-brown leather apron**. Prop: a small shoemaker's hammer (or big round-tipped scissors on page 2). On page 3 only:
  a white nightshirt and a **blue nightcap**.
- **Greta:** his wife, a little shorter and rounder than Hans; **grey hair in a bun**, rosy cheeks, kind smile. She
  wears a **leaf-green dress with a white apron and a red knitted shawl** over her shoulders. Prop: a red
  **pincushion on her wrist**. On page 3 only: a white nightgown and a **pink nightcap**.
- **Nib:** a girl elf, **about the height of a shoe** (much smaller than the cat), the same size as Nob; pointy ears,
  rosy cheeks, **two short brown plaits**. She wears a **pointy red hat**. Pages 9-13: a thin, **oatmeal-coloured
  tunic with one blue patch on the front**, bare feet. Pages 14-15 (the gift): a **little red coat with gold buttons**,
  a **yellow woolly scarf**, tiny brown boots, and still the pointy red hat. Prop: a tiny hammer (page 10).
- **Nob:** a boy elf, the same size as Nib; pointy ears, a round button nose, **short curly ginger hair**. He wears a
  **pointy green hat**. Pages 9-13: a thin, **oatmeal-coloured tunic with one orange patch on the front**, bare feet.
  Pages 14-15: a **little green coat with gold buttons**, a **blue woolly scarf**, tiny brown boots, and still the
  pointy green hat. Prop: a tiny needle and thread (page 10).
  Look-alike rule for the elves: **red hat and plaits = Nib, green hat and curly ginger hair = Nob**, in every prompt.
- **The cat** (no name, never speaks): a round, sleepy **ginger cat with a white tip on its tail**, no collar; bigger
  than both elves together. In every picture indoors (it adds a third character to the Hans-and-Greta pages).
- **The lady** (no name, never speaks; page 5 only, and one of the customers on page 6): a tall lady in a **purple
  coat** and a little purple hat.
- **Customers** (page 6 only, no names): two or three townsfolk in winter coats (not purple, red or green: brown, grey,
  blue), one of them a small child.

## Places (the same in every picture)

- **The shop and workshop:** one cosy room on the ground floor of a narrow timbered house. A long, sturdy **wooden
  workbench** in the middle with tools and leather; wooden shelves of shoes along the back wall; a **big, saggy
  armchair with a patchwork blanket** in the corner; **one wide window with a single pane** showing the snowy street; a
  wooden front door with a **small round-topped cat flap** at the bottom. Floorboards. **No fireplace, stove, candle,
  oil lamp or flame anywhere.** Daytime: pale winter daylight; evening: blue dusk through the window; night: silver
  moonlight through the window falling on the workbench.
- **The bedroom upstairs** (page 3 only): a sloping ceiling, a wooden bed with a patchwork quilt, a small window with
  the moon and falling snow. No candle.
- **The snowy street** (page 15): the shop's front door open onto a quiet street of timbered houses under deep snow,
  a full moon, stars.

## Picture plan

Every page: the whole scene sits in the top 70% of the picture; the bottom 30% is plain floorboards, quilt or snow with
nothing important in it. No text, letters, words, sound effects or writing anywhere ("Tap, tap, tap", "Stitch" are
never drawn in; no shop sign with letters). Every page has **three or more characters** (Gemini refuses some 1-2
character scenes): the cat is in every indoor picture. The elves are tiny: always draw them on the workbench, the
floor near the cat flap or the doorstep, never as big as the cat. Every picture is **new** (no pictures exist).

The last item is ticked for the plan: every look is fixed in the character lines above, and the illustrator draws one

Notes for the illustrator:

- The elves have **two outfits**: thin oatmeal tunics with one patch and bare feet on pages 9-13 (and the cover); red
  and green coats with gold buttons, yellow and blue scarves and tiny brown boots on pages 14-15. Name which one in
  every prompt; the hats never change. The tunics are plain and a little worn, never torn rags, and nobody is naked.
- Pages 8-11 and 13-14 are all at night in the same workshop: keep them apart by the action and where the elves are
  (none yet, coming through the cat flap, working, being thanked, finding presents, dancing in new clothes).
- Hans and Greta are always **openly** in the room: sitting in the armchair or standing at the bench, never behind a
  curtain, door or cupboard, never peeking.
- No fireplace, stove, candle, oil lamp, kettle or flame anywhere; moonlight only at night.
- Pages 3 and 15 are the only pages away from the workshop. Page 3 (asleep in bed) is the only one in nightclothes; on
  page 15 they are in their day clothes at the door (the book ends there, with no bedroom epilogue).
- Ask for "exactly two people, two elves and one cat, each appearing once" (adjust per page), and "no text, letters,
  words, sound effects or writing anywhere".
