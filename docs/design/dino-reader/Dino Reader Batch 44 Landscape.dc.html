Landscape for every screen, phone and tablet
Drawn on top of Batch 43 (Grown-ups pill, corner, Done/Continue). Four rules carry every screen, so each page needs one layout function, not a hand-made layout: split, don’t stretch (a picture or list on the left, the working part on the right); the header becomes a side rail where a page has a pinned button; reading controls move to a right-hand rail on phones; and nothing floats over a picture except the small back-and-view chip. Frames 110–132. Phone frames are 760 × 360 dp, tablet frames 1164 × 804 dp (iPad Air / 11″ Android at 1×).
For the review
1 · Which screen hurt most? The slice builds that one first. My guess is reading (113 replaces 92, where the controls sat on the art and the picture was cropped to 40%).
2 · Shelf direction. On a phone the kids’ shelf scrolls sideways in landscape (one row of big covers, 110) and down in portrait. Tablets keep a grid. OK, or always down?
3 · Dark and right-to-left follow the Batch 43 answers; only the reading screen (which already has night mode) is drawn dark here (131).
Layout rules for Core (pure, unit-tested)
IsLandscape(w,h): w > h. Phone vs tablet by the short side: under 600 dp is a phone.
ShelfLayout: phone landscape → one row, sideways, cover height = h − 120; otherwise a grid, columns = clamp(⌊w ÷ 190⌋, 2, 6).
SplitLayout: pages with a picture or a list beside their work. Left = picture fitted to full height, at most 45% of w; list panes 300 dp (phone) / 360 dp (tablet).
ReadingControls: right rail when h < 480 dp, else a row under the text. Play 68 / 84, previous and next 52 / 64.
PinnedButton: in landscape, a page’s pinned bottom button moves to the bottom of its side rail.
Rotation keeps the reading position, a recording in progress, the wizard step and gate progress (state lives in the view models, not the layouts).
