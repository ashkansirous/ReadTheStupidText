Galaxy Z Fold8: the cover screen and the open book
The same screens as the iPhone Duo page, on the Fold8. Both phones turned out the same shape: a narrow-ish portrait cover and a landscape inner screen. So the rule is the same: the cover is under 600 dp wide, so it uses the phone layouts (it is as wide as a Pixel 10 Pro, P4); the inner screen is over 600 dp, so it uses the rail and two-pane layouts, and reading becomes an open book with the fold as the gutter. Android draws edge to edge, so backgrounds run under the status bar and the gesture handle, as in P4.
What Samsung has published (Fold8): a 5.5-inch cover screen at 10:16 (portrait) and a 7.6-inch inner screen at 4:3, landscape (“wider, shorter”); both QHD+; the cover has a camera hole. The Fold8 Ultra is a different, bigger phone; its screen sizes were not on the pages read, so it is not drawn.
Not published (checked 2026-11-16): pixel counts, dp sizes, where the cameras sit and the inner screen’s camera type. The sizes drawn are estimates from the diagonals and ratios at about 141 dp per inch: cover about 411 × 658 dp, inner about 857 × 643 dp. Check them on the device or the emulator profile when we have one.
What that means for Dino: the cover is P4’s width but 658 tall instead of 915, so it shows less down, as on the Duo. The inner screen is almost the Duo’s inner screen (857 × 643 against 867 × 613), so its frames are the Duo’s with Android’s chrome. A camera hole is drawn on the cover only.

The phone layouts, shorter
Under 600 wide: tabs along the bottom, one column, the coral button at the foot, edge to edge with a 24 dp gesture inset. Closing the phone while reading keeps the same page and moves it here.

Questions for the review
1. Fold8 Ultra. Samsung also sells a Fold8 Ultra, a bigger phone. Its screen sizes weren’t on the pages I could read. Want it drawn too once its sizes are checked? The same split almost certainly applies.
2. Multi-window. On the inner screen Dino can run beside another app. Half of 857 dp is about 428, under 600, so Dino shows its cover-screen layouts there. Worth a device check, no extra design.
