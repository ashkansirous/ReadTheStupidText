One gear for grown-ups, story languages for the kid, a calmer reading screen
A gear on the shelf, visible in kids’ mode too, opens the gate and then Settings. Each setting gets its own page (Slice 172). The gear is the only grown-up way in. Settings › Books opens grown-up mode, which is now only for Add from your own books, editing books and the Store (formerly “Free stories”). Parents can pick up to two extra story languages, and the child switches between them from the reading page (Slice 173). The reading page drops the parents’ button and gets a flat play button and a real landscape layout. Back never closes the app by surprise (Slice 174). Frames 79–94. This file is the mock that Slice 174 waits on.

There is no top bar, no title and no page dots. The left 40% holds the picture, cropped to fit from the top (the bottom 30% of the art is free space for words, so losing it costs nothing), and the controls float over the picture’s bottom part, side by side as in portrait: previous 52, play 68, next 52. Since that part of the art is kept free for words, the buttons never hide anything important. The text gets the whole right side, full height, at 23 px. Back and the view switch float over the picture’s top corner on a white chip, so they show over any art, and the language flag (88) joins them when it’s shown. The left inset clears the notch. The text auto-scrolls as in 91. This applies to picture books and photo books alike. For photo books, whose words may be anywhere, it is a proposal to switch to fit-whole instead.
