## Why

The store's stories are text only. The user wants a cover and a picture on every page. There is no
image API, only the Gemini web app. Doing it by hand means generating character sheets, downloading
them, opening a new chat and re-explaining the story for every page, which is too much work. Claude
drives Gemini in the user's signed-in Chrome instead. The user's thread
(https://gemini.google.com/app/5bdec8c40a5d27ba, "3 little pigs characters") already has a Pim sheet,
a Wolf portrait and sheet, and a line-up of Pip, Pop, Pim and Wolf. This slice finishes that book first,
as the pilot that proves the loop.

The user also wants the four bundled starters (Good Enough, The Three Little Pigs, The Gingerbread Man,
The Tortoise and the Hare) in the store, so anyone who deletes one can download it again.

## Decisions (2026-10-25, agreed with the user)

- **Decision 125: Claude illustrates the books by driving the Gemini web app in the user's Chrome.**
  The user approved, once and for all, saving Gemini's pictures into a working folder and committing
  them to the repo. Per-chat procedure (revised 2026-10-25 after the pigs pilot,
  where one long chat mixed scenes and outfits: wrong-scene wolf, trousers on pigs, a 4th pig): one
  character chat makes the sheets and the line-up; each picture then comes from a fresh page chat with
  the saved line-up pasted in and every outfit spelled out (including what they do not wear), at most
  3 pictures per chat, and one per chat once a chat repeats an earlier scene. The free tier has a daily
  image cap: when Gemini refuses, stop and resume next day.
- **Decision 126: one house style for every book.** "Children's picture book illustration, soft
  watercolour and coloured pencil, warm gentle colours, simple rounded shapes, no text". The exact
  style line and each book's character descriptions are kept in `docs/design/story-art/<stem>.md`.
- **Decision 127: pictures are 3:4 portrait with no text in them.** Upright, the picture fills the page
  and the text panel covers up to 58% from the bottom, so every page prompt asks for the characters and
  action in the top half. Covers are text-free; the title comes from our code, not from Gemini (it
  misspells, and one picture serves all 9 languages).
- **Decision 128: the bundled starters are also store stories, with the same package id.** A user who
  deleted one sees it in the store and downloads it back. The installed copy and the store copy are the
  same book.
- This reverses the Batch 10 out-of-scope item "Illustrations generated by AI" and supersedes the
  public-domain illustration follow-up of Slice 102 / Decision 106.

## Gentle stories (added 2026-10-25, after the user's review of the first pictures)

- **Decision 130: no Dino book shows harm or pain, in its text or its pictures, whatever the classic
  tale says.** Readers are 3-9. Nobody is eaten, burnt, boiled, drowned, beaten, killed, falls to
  their death or is threatened with any of that; no fire or hot water near a character; no "licks his
  lips" or other hints of eating someone. Conflicts end gently: a splash of cold bubbly water, a
  fright that turns into a laugh, a sorry, running home, a new friend. Keep each story recognisable
  and its page breaks (`---`) where they are, so picture counts stay right.
- Audit and rewrite all 14 books (the 4 starters in `StarterTexts.cs`, the 10 store stories in
  `StoreTexts.cs`), then update every changed piece in `Starters/translations/<stem>.<lang>.json`
  (13 languages) and rebuild: `DINO_BUILD_STARTERS=1` for the bundled starters and
  `DINO_BUILD_STORE=1` for the store, store stories as `version: 3`.
- The Three Little Pigs page 5: the Wolf slides down the chimney into a big tub of cold, bubbly bath
  water, sneezes "Achoo!", climbs out soggy and soapy, runs home and never blows down a house again.
  Page 2: "He licked his lips" becomes "He grinned".
- Every generated picture passes the full-size check in the `create-dino-story` skill before it is
  saved: exact character count and outfits, the scene matches the page, nothing hurt, scary or on
  fire, no text, action high on the page.
