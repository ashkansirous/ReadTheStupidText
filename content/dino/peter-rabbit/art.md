# The Tale of Peter Rabbit: art record

Store story (stem `peter-rabbit`, version 8). Retold as 15 pages with one action each
(`dino-storytelling` skill, Slice 121). Beatrix Potter's 1902 text is public domain; this retelling changes the plot a
lot, so the credit is now `Beatrix Potter, 1902 (retold)`. **Art status (2026-10-26): cover and all 15 pictures drawn and accepted, full review PASS on 2026-10-26 (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, 1792x2400 originals, first picture about 20:30).** The
cover and all 15 pages are new; the text-only version 5 had none.

Version 8 (Slice 146 Waves B+C and Slice 156, 2026-10-27): English reworded in the spoken register ("I'm off to the shops",
"Okay?", "off ran Flopsy...", "Uh-oh!", "way too much", "He was so hot!", "I should've listened", "dressed up his scarecrow",
"And there it was", "I'm really sorry, Mum. Next time, I'll listen. I promise!", "Night-night") and every sound word its own
triple piece (Robin's line is now `"Tweet, tweet, tweet!" said Robin. "Of course I can! ..."`, so the tweet is no longer glued
to "Of course I can"). Page 1 gains "Mrs. Rabbit gave his cheek a little pat." (her hand at his face in the existing picture; the reviewer chose it over "did up his top button"). Same 15 page
breaks and the same one action per page: **every page keeps its picture** (checked against the contact sheet on 2026-10-27).
Pages are 31-60 words (page 7 trimmed: "He forgot his jacket and shoes!"). The 12 other languages were redone for every reworded piece in the same
register with their own triples (French tags now `a dit ...` in the passé composé, Finnish dialogue in spoken `mä`/`sä`
forms, Chinese hopping `蹦蹦跳` and grumbling `嘟囔` instead of lone characters, Japanese robin `ピーチク`); Farsi changed only
where the meaning moved (no vowel marks; `وای وای وای` for uh-oh, `قول می‌دم`, `می‌دونی چی شد؟` was dropped with the English
"And guess what?").

**Gentle retelling (Decision 130):** in Potter, Peter's father "was put in a pie by Mrs. McGregor", Mr. McGregor chases
Peter with a rake shouting "Stop thief!", Peter is caught by his buttons in a gooseberry net, Mr. McGregor tries to trap
him under a sieve, Peter hides soaking wet in a watering can, sneezes and is chased again, and a white cat watches the
goldfish pond. None of that is here:
- **No father and no pie.** Mr. Rabbit is never mentioned; Mrs. Rabbit is named as their mum on page 1, and her reason is simply "It's his garden, not ours."
- **Mr. McGregor never sees Peter, never chases him and holds no tool** (no rake, hoe, sieve or net; only his
  wheelbarrow). He is grumpy about his nibbled lettuces ("Grumble, grumble, grumble!"), then finds the jacket and shoes,
  dresses his scarecrow in them, and smiles for the first time all day.
- **Peter loses his clothes by forgetting them**, not by tearing free: very hot, he hangs his jacket on a bean pole and
  puts his shoes beside it, then rests under a rhubarb leaf, and forgets them when he sets off to find the gate. He
  can't take them back off the scarecrow because "the scarecrow was much too tall, and Peter was much too small".
- **Lost, not in danger.** The vegetables are tall and every path looks the same. Potter's mouse with a pea in her mouth
  stays (she can only shake her head); the cat and the pond are gone. Peter hides behind a cabbage once, keeping "as
  still as a stone", while Mr. McGregor grumbles past; nothing happens to him.
- **A friendly robin leads him out** (Potter's sparrows cheer him on; here two sparrows watch, and a robin guides him
  along a cat-free path, past the scarecrow, to the gate).
- **The tummy ache is gentle:** "very full, and very funny". At bedtime Mum gives him "a little cup of camomile tea" (no
  kettle, steam, spoon of medicine or fire), and his sisters have bread and milk and blackberries.
- Words: "naughty", "thief", "pie" and "fright" are not used; "hurt" and "pie" are on `GentleTextTests`' banned list.

**Why everyone does what they do:**
- **Mrs. Rabbit** goes to the shops and lets the children play nearby. She tells them to stay out of Mr. McGregor's garden
  because it belongs to him ("It's his garden, not ours"), the rule a 4-year-old understands. When Peter comes home
  without his clothes she hugs him first and asks after, and she puts him to bed with camomile tea because his tummy
  still feels funny.
- **Peter** goes to the garden because he wants the yummy vegetables more than blackberries (he says so on page 3). He eats
  too much (lettuce, beans, radishes: the three-step build-up), so his tummy feels funny and he is hot; that is why he
  takes off his jacket and shoes and rests. He gets lost because the bean plants are taller than he is. Sitting by the
  watering can he remembers Mum's words, and he knows by himself that he should have listened. When the robin comes he asks for help politely ("Please, can you help me find the
  gate?"), the way a lost child should. He waves goodbye to his clothes on the scarecrow because it is far too tall for
  him to reach them (he is not upset), and at home he says sorry and promises to listen next time.
- **Flopsy, Mopsy and Cotton-tail** do as Mum said and pick blackberries in the lane. Flopsy, the eldest, saves Peter
  some for tomorrow, because sisters look after each other.
- **Mr. McGregor** is grumpy because someone has nibbled his lettuces. He is not unkind: he finds a tiny jacket and tiny
  shoes, thinks they are just right for his scarecrow, and it makes him smile. He goes in for lunch, which is why the
  robin can lead Peter out quietly.
- **The two sparrows** shake their heads at the gate because they know whose garden it is (the lesson, without words).
- **The mouse** cannot answer Peter because her mouth is full of a big pea (Potter's joke, kept).
- **Robin** is a garden robin who knows every path; when the lost little rabbit asks for help he says "Of course I
  can", leads him to the gate, then tells him to hop straight home.

**Refrains (identical every time):**
- `"But stay out of Mr. McGregor's garden!" said Mrs. Rabbit.` on page 2 (Mum says it) and page 8 (Peter remembers it,
  in Mum's voice).
- `Lippity, lippity, lippity!` (Potter's own hopping word) on pages 3, 12 and 14: off to the garden, following Robin,
  hopping home.
- `Wiggle, wiggle, wiggle!` on page 4 (under the gate, in) and page 13 (under the gate, out).
- Other sound words are triples too (Decision 151): `Munch, munch, munch!`, `Yum, yum, yum!`, `Creak, creak, creak!`,
  `Grumble, grumble, grumble!`, `Well, well, well!`, `Tweet, tweet, tweet!`, each its own piece (since version 8 the tweet is
  Robin's first line on its own). Languages other than en/es/zh/fa are untested for length: flag the sound words for a listen.

**Cast and voices (Slice 156, the book's own choices, `StarterVoices.cs`):** Mrs. Rabbit, Peter, Mr. McGregor, Robin, Flopsy
(`book.json` `characters`; Mopsy and Cotton-tail never speak, so they are not in the cast). **Mrs. Rabbit and Flopsy are "she";
Peter, Mr. McGregor and Robin are "he"** (her basket, his mum, behind him, he waved his wing).
- **Mrs. Rabbit** (woman, adult rabbit; warm and soft) suggests `mummy`.
- **Peter** (boy, child rabbit; bouncy and bright, the cheeky one) suggests `piglet` (Cheeky Piglet).
- **Mr. McGregor** (man, old, a person; gruff but warm) suggests `troll` (Grumpy Troll); Farsi has no troll, so its traits pick
  the grumpy gardener (باغبان اخمو).
- **Robin** (boy, young bird; bright and bouncy) suggests `jester` (Jolly Jester, English only); every other language matches
  him by traits, which gives the chick (Poussin Piou, Pollito Pío...), lamb2 in Farsi and piglet3 in Chinese. `puppy` was tried
  and dropped: it put little Robin on the deep Santa voice in Spanish and Portuguese, worse than sharing am_puck with Peter in
  English (Bouncy against Peter's Small).
- **Flopsy** (girl, young rabbit; bright and warm) suggests `bee`.

**Style:** Children's picture book illustration, soft watercolour and coloured pencil, warm gentle colours, simple
rounded shapes, no text or letters anywhere. Portrait 3:4.

## Not Potter's pictures (read before any prompt)

- **Never put "Peter Rabbit", "Beatrix Potter", "Tale of Peter Rabbit", "Mr. McGregor", "Flopsy", "Mopsy" or
  "Cotton-tail" in a Gemini prompt.** Gemini refuses well-known character names ("third-party content providers",
  Gingerbread Man 2026-10-26). Describe them: "the little brown rabbit boy", "the mother rabbit", "the three rabbit
  sisters (grey in lavender, golden in yellow, white in peach)", "the old gardener", "the robin".
- Don't echo the famous 1902 illustrations: **no blue jacket with brass buttons** (Peter's is grass green with yellow
  buttons, plus red shoes), **no blue-and-white striped dress or white apron** on the mother (plum dress, straw hat),
  **no red cloaks** on the sisters (pinafores in three colours), **no white beard** on the gardener (moustache only,
  cap, mustard shirt, brown dungarees). The rabbits are cute, rounded picture-book animals with big heads and eyes,
  standing on their hind legs, not naturalistic studies; no Victorian vignette framing.
- No famous scenes: no rabbit squeezing under a gate with only his back legs showing in Potter's pose, no rabbit in a
  watering can, no gooseberry net, no sieve, no rake, no cat by a pond.

## Characters (canonical until a line-up is accepted; then rewrite from the line-up)

- **Peter (the little rabbit boy):** the smallest rabbit in the book; soft warm sandy-brown fur, cream tummy, a white
  fluffy tail, long ears with pink insides, big dark eyes. **Cover and pages 1-5:** a **grass-green jacket with three round
  yellow buttons** and **little red shoes**; nothing else (no trousers, no hat, no scarf). **Pages 6-15 (page 10 included): no jacket
  and no shoes**, just his fur. In bed on page 15 he is under a quilt. The only rabbit boy.
- **Mrs. Rabbit (the mother rabbit):** the biggest rabbit, about twice Peter's height; soft grey-brown fur, darker than
  Peter's; a **plum-purple dress** to her ankles; outdoors (pages 1, 2, 14) a **straw sun hat with a yellow ribbon**; her
  prop is a **wicker basket with a red-checked cloth** (pages 2 and 14). Indoors (page 15) no hat. Always kind-faced,
  never stern; she never points at or wags a finger at anyone.
- **Flopsy:** the eldest sister, a little taller than Peter; **light grey fur**; a **lavender pinafore dress** with a white
  collar; a small wicker basket for blackberries.
- **Mopsy:** the same height as Flopsy; **golden-tan fur**; a **sunflower-yellow pinafore dress** with a white daisy on the
  pocket; a small wicker basket.
- **Cotton-tail:** the youngest sister, a little taller than Peter and shorter than the others; **snow-white fur** with
  pale pink ears and an extra-fluffy tail; a **peach-pink pinafore dress**; a small wicker basket.
- **Mr. McGregor (the old gardener):** a tall, round-tummied older man (about five times Peter's height), rosy cheeks,
  **bushy white eyebrows and a big white moustache, no beard**; a **flat brown cap**, a **mustard-yellow shirt** with
  rolled-up sleeves, **brown dungarees**, green wellington boots. His only prop is a **wooden wheelbarrow** with a few
  cabbages in it. **He never holds a rake, hoe, fork, spade, sieve, net or any tool.** Grumpy means a frown with his mouth
  closed, no teeth showing; on page 10 a small smile.
- **Robin:** a small, round European robin with an **orange-red breast and face**, olive-brown back and wings, thin legs;
  about the size of Peter's head. No clothes.
- **The mouse:** a tiny brown field mouse, about the size of Peter's paw, pink ears and a long thin tail. No clothes. On
  page 7 a big green pea in her mouth.
- **The two sparrows:** small brown-and-grey house sparrows, always the two of them when both are there. No clothes.
- **The scarecrow (a thing, not a character):** a wooden cross-pole with a round straw-stuffed sack head with a
  **stitched smile** (friendly, never spooky), an old straw hat and straw poking out of stick arms. It appears only on
  pages 10 and 12, **wearing Peter's green jacket**, with the red shoes hanging from its stick arms.

There are no other characters: no father rabbit, no cat, no Mrs. McGregor, no goldfish, no other people.

## Places (the same in every picture)

- **The burrow:** a round wooden door in a grassy sand bank between the roots of **one big fir tree** at the edge of a
  wood, wildflowers in front. Pages 1, 2 and 14. **Inside** (page 15): a cosy round room with earthy walls, a small bed
  with a patchwork quilt, a little round wooden table with three stools, **one round window with a single pane** showing
  the dusk sky. **No fireplace, stove, kettle, candle or flame anywhere**; the light is a soft evening glow.
- **The lane:** a dirt lane along a hedge of blackberry bushes; in the distance a red-brick wall with a green gate.
  Page 3.
- **The garden:** inside a red-brick wall; **one green wooden gate with a small gap under it**; neat rows of lettuces,
  radishes, carrots, onions and big cabbages, **tall runner beans on poles** (taller than Peter), huge rhubarb leaves,
  narrow gravel paths, a green watering can, a few low bushes. Pages 4-13 (page 13 from the lane side of the gate). No
  pond, no shed, no net, no cold frame.
