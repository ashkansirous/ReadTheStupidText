# The Three Billy Goats Gruff: art record

Store story (stem `billy-goats-gruff`, version 8; v8 is the spoken-language rewrite and the book's own voices, Slices 146 and 156). Retold as 14 pages with one
action each (`dino-storytelling` skill). **Art status (2026-10-25): all 14 pages and the cover drawn and prepared into the repo. Pages 10, 11, 13 and 14 redrawn or drawn after the Google AI Pro upgrade; the cover re-roll was tried and rejected, so the current cover is kept. The book is complete and passed the full-mode review.** Pages 8-9 show a rope rail (accepted by the reviewer); every other page has the wooden post-and-rail.

**Gentle ending (Decision 130):** in the classic tale Big Billy butts the Troll into the river and he is
swept away. Here nobody pushes anyone: the Troll stamps on the wet old boards, wobbles and slides off the
low bridge into shallow, cool water that only reaches his tummy. A fish tickles his toes, everyone laughs,
Big Billy asks if he is all right, and the lonely Troll is invited to the meadow and becomes their friend.
The Troll's refrain is "Nobody crosses my bridge without a stamping match!" (never a threat to eat anyone):
the small goats are too small to stamp and send him to their bigger brother, and on page 8 he stamps his
match against Big Billy, who waits his turn, until the slippery boards tip the Troll into the water.

**Style:** Children's picture book illustration, soft watercolour and coloured pencil, warm gentle
colours, simple rounded shapes, no text or letters anywhere. Portrait 3:4. Sizes as drawn: cover and pages 1-9, 12 at 896x1200 (free plan); pages 10, 11, 13, 14 at 1792x2400 (after the Pro upgrade); all prepared to 896x1200.

## Characters (canonical, from the accepted line-up)

Line-up: [`billy-goats-gruff/characters/lineup.jpg`](billy-goats-gruff/characters/lineup.jpg) (character chat: https://gemini.google.com/app/5f7043dc65bf60b2; no separate sheets were drawn, the line-up alone held the looks; attempt 2 removed two small teeth from the Troll's frown). In the line-up the Troll is about as tall as Big Billy (his hair reaches Big Billy's horns) but much rounder; he wears only the waistcoat: bare tummy, bare legs, bare feet. Every page prompt must say "no trousers, no shorts": Gemini adds ragged trousers to the Troll whenever he stamps or sits.

- **Little Billy:** small white kid goat, about half Big Billy's height; tiny nub horns, no beard; thin
  red collar with a small round gold bell (his prop). The smallest; the only one with a bell.
- **Middle Billy:** medium light-brown goat, about two thirds of Big Billy's height; short curved horns,
  small beard; blue neckerchief with white spots (his prop). No bell, no scarf.
- **Big Billy:** large grey-brown goat, the tallest goat; big curly horns, long white beard; green
  knitted scarf (his prop). No bell, no neckerchief. Calm and kind, never charging or head-down.
- **Troll:** round, friendly-looking troll, head level with Big Billy's head (as drawn on pages 8 and 12); mossy-green skin, big
  round eyes like saucers, a long orange carrot-shaped nose, shaggy brown hair; patched brown waistcoat,
  bare feet. Empty hands: no stick, club or tool (nothing that reads as a weapon). Grumpy means a frown and
  folded arms, never bared teeth, claws or a raised fist.

## Places (the same in every picture)

- **The hill:** a gentle hill with short, patchy yellow-green grass, on the near side of the river.
- **The bridge:** a low wooden plank bridge with a simple rail, only a little above the water, its old
  boards a bit mossy. Its near (hill-side) end rests over a dry pebbly bank; the Troll's flat stone sits
  there in its shade, and when he stands on that bank the bridge deck is at his chest height.
- **The river:** shallow, clear and slow, with round pebbles; never deep, fast or foaming.
- **The meadow:** a bright green meadow full of long grass and small flowers on the far side.

## Spoken-language rewrite (v8, 2026-10-27, Slices 146 and 156)

The English was rewritten in spoken, bedtime words (Decision 152): contractions ("Who's that trip-trapping over my bridge?", "It's only me"),
"Uh-oh!", "Whoops!", "yummy green grass", "teeny-tiny voice", "Oh, all right then, off you go!" (now the same both times), and the lone
"Splash!" became "Splash, splash, splash!" (Decision 151; one piece, so no language reads a lone short sound). Page breaks and each page's
one action are unchanged, so **every page and the cover keep their picture** (none needs redrawing). The other 12 languages were redone in
the same register with their own traditional names and endings (Bukkene Bruse, Bockarna Bruse, Kolme pukkia, 山妖 and 嘎啦嘎啦 in Chinese,
colorín colorado, cric crac, Вот и сказке конец, وتوتة توتة خلصت الحدوتة, めでたし); Farsi kept its Wave A text and changed only the
pieces whose meaning changed. Sounds in the untested languages (all but en, es, zh, fa) use the repeat form and want a listen.

## Voices (Slice 156: the book's own picks, `StarterVoices`)

The picker had cast the Troll as the Rumbly Dragon and the three brothers as girls' lamb and sheep voices (Big Billy, "in a big, deep
voice", was the sleepy sheep). The book now suggests:

- **Little Billy:** `piglet` (Cheeky Piglet, small and quick); boy, child, squeaky and bright. Goat (bleats from the book animal).
- **Middle Billy:** `knight` (Brave Knight, bold and clear, the Normal flavour); boy, young, bold and bright. Goat.
- **Big Billy:** `giant` (Friendly Giant, big, deep and slow); male, adult, booming and warm. Goat.
- **Troll:** `troll` (Grumpy Troll, gruff and low); male, adult, a troll, gruff. Not an animal. Farsi has no troll character, so its
  trait match picks (today the wolf voice).

Every language plays all four with a male voice (`TheThreeBillyGoatsGruff_GivesEachCharacterAVoiceOfTheirGender_InEveryLanguagesRoster`).
